# Email Template Framework

---

## CORE PRINCIPLE

Don't send generic emails. Customize 1-2 lines per prospect to show you know their business.

Generic: "You need a website" (low response)
Customized: "Your salon's Instagram is beautiful but Google doesn't know about you" (higher response)

---

## TEMPLATE STRUCTURE (Universal)

Every email has the same parts:

1. **Subject line** (compelling, specific)
2. **Hook** (why you're writing, personalized to them)
3. **Problem** (what you solve)
4. **Solution** (what you offer)
5. **CTA** (one specific next step)
6. **Signature** (you, contact, company)

**Length:** 4-6 sentences (mobile-readable)

---

## TEMPLATE A: NO WEBSITE

**Use for:** Prospects with zero online presence

**Subject Lines (pick one):**
- "Quick question — [Business Name]"
- "[Business Name] is missing out"
- "Why [Business Type] need websites in 2026"

> We build custom websites for [business type plural] that show your work, build trust, and help people find you. Usually takes 2-3 weeks and costs €800-€1,500.

> Worth a quick 15-min call to see if it makes sense for you?

> [Your name]
> [Your phone]
> [Your email]
> [Company]

## CUSTOMIZATION FORMULA

**High-impact personalization (takes 30 seconds per email):**

For **[Specific observation]**, mention:
- Something specific you saw on their Instagram/Google Maps/website
- A pain point relevant to their industry
- A compliment + improvement angle

Examples by industry:

**Hair Salon:**
- ✅ "Your Instagram shows incredible work — but Google doesn't know about you"
- ✅ "Your portfolio is stunning but you're only visible to walk-ins"
- ✅ "No online booking system means you're answering calls all day"

**Massage/Wellness:**
- ✅ "People want to know who they're trusting before booking — your site should show that"
- ✅ "Your reviews are great; a website would let people find you first"
- ✅ "No website = clients booking with competitors instead"

**Fitness:**
- ✅ "Class schedule buried in Instagram comments instead of searchable online"
- ✅ "No way for people to book classes except calling you"
- ✅ "You're using Facebook for scheduling when you could have a real system"

**Restaurants:**
- ✅ "People Google your hours before coming — no website = they go somewhere else"
- ✅ "Menu only on Instagram; customers can't share it with friends"
- ✅ "No reservations system; bookings happening via DMs"

**Retail:**
- ✅ "Beautiful store but people don't know you exist unless they drive by"
- ✅ "Instagram followers but no way to direct them to your location"
- ✅ "No product showcase online; only walk-in discovery"

## SUBJECT LINE FORMULAS

**High open rate subjects:**

1. **Curiosity:** "Quick question — [Business Name]"
2. **Observation:** "[Business Name] is missing out"
3. **Opportunity:** "Opportunity for [Business Name]"
4. **Specific angle:** "Why [Business Type] need [specific thing]"
5. **Direct:** "[Business Name]'s website could be better"

**Low open rate subjects (avoid):**
- ❌ "Website Design" (generic, spam-like)
- ❌ "SEO Services" (immediately deleted)
- ❌ "Let's work together" (vague)
- ❌ "Important: Read this" (scammy)

---

## CALL-TO-ACTION FORMULAS

**High conversion CTAs:**

1. **Low-friction:** "Worth a quick 15-min call?"
2. **Specific:** "Can I send you a quick mockup of what this could look like?"
3. **No-pressure:** "No obligation — just a conversation to see if it makes sense"
4. **Action:** "Reply with your best time to chat and I'll send you a calendar link"
5. **Time-bound:** "Free consultation this week? I have [Day] or [Day] available"

**Low conversion CTAs (avoid):**
- ❌ "Let me know if interested"
- ❌ "Call us" (no specific ask)
- ❌ "Visit our website" (defeats purpose)
- ❌ "Tell me about your budget" (too forward too soon)

---

## A/B TESTING ANGLES

If you're sending lots of emails, test different angles:

**A Variation:** Lead with problem ("Google search = you don't exist")
**B Variation:** Lead with solution ("We build custom websites for salons")

Track which gets higher response rate, then use winner for remaining prospects.

**A Variation:** Social proof ("We just built one for [similar business]")
**B Variation:** Urgency ("Google ranking algorithm changes this month")

Same approach.

## LANGUAGE & TONE BY INDUSTRY

**Hair Salons / Beauty:**
- Tone: Warm, visual, appreciative of their aesthetic
- Reference: Instagram, portfolio, "your work"
- Example: "Your salon's Instagram is stunning — let's get you found when people search"

**Massage / Wellness:**
- Tone: Professional, trustworthy, client-focused
- Reference: Client trust, professionalism, reviews
- Example: "People want to know they're trusting the right person — your site should show that"

**Fitness / Gyms:**
- Tone: Results-oriented, community-focused
- Reference: Classes, schedules, community, member acquisition
- Example: "Your classes are full but people can't find you online — that's leaving money on the table"

**Restaurants / Cafes:**
- Tone: Casual, customer-journey focused
- Reference: Hours, menu, location, dining experience
- Example: "People Google your hours before visiting — you should be discoverable"

**Trades (Carpenters, Electricians):**
- Tone: Professional, practical, business-focused
- Reference: Portfolio, project quality, credibility
- Example: "Your past work speaks for itself — let's show it to future clients"

**Retail:**
- Tone: Sales-minded, discovery-focused
- Reference: Customers, browsing, curation
- Example: "Your store is beautiful but most people won't find you unless they're driving by"

## FOLLOW-UP SEQUENCE (If no response)

**Email 1 (Day 1):** Initial contact (above templates)

**Email 2 (Day 7, if no response):**
> Hi [Name],

> Just following up on my previous note. I know you're busy — thought I'd check in one more time.

> If now's not the right time, totally understand. Just reach out if you want to chat about how we can help [Business Name] get found online.

> [Your name]

**Email 3 (Day 14, if no response):**
> Final note, then I'll leave you alone:

> [Business Name] has great word-of-mouth, but most online searches aren't finding you. That's potential revenue on the table.

> If you ever want to explore options, just reply.

> [Your name]

**After Email 3:** Stop (don't spam). Mark as "no response" in tracking and move on.

**Response rate expectation:**
- Email 1: 5-10% response (15-20% for Tier 1)
- Email 2: 2-5% additional response
- Email 3: 1-2% additional response
- Total: 8-17% response rate from unsolicited outreach (Tier 1 = higher)

## TRACKING RESPONSES

Create spreadsheet:

```
Date Sent | Prospect | Email Sent | Subject | Template Used | Response (Y/N) | Response Type | Date Responded | Follow-up Needed | Status
```

**Response types to log:**
- ✅ Interest ("Tell me more")
- ⚠️ Maybe ("Could work, but...")
- ❌ Not interested ("No thanks")
- ⏱️ Wrong person ("Not the decision maker")
- ⏰ Wrong timing ("Maybe in 6 months")

Use this data to refine messaging and timing.

## ANTI-PATTERNS (What NOT to do)

❌ **Generic templates sent as-is**
- Result: Spam folder, 1-2% response

✅ **Customized templates** (one personal line per email)
- Result: Inbox, 10-20% response

---

❌ **Asking for the sale immediately**
- "Let's build your website"
- Result: Ignored

✅ **Asking for a conversation**
- "Quick 15-min call to see if it makes sense?"
- Result: Some say yes

---

❌ **Long emails**
- 3+ paragraphs, lots of details
- Result: Low open rate

✅ **Short emails**
- 4-6 sentences, mobile-friendly
- Result: Higher open rate

---

❌ **Hard sell**
- "Your website is terrible"
- Result: Defensive, ignore

✅ **Soft approach**
- "Your Instagram is great; Google search is the opportunity"
- Result: Receptive

---

## FINAL RULES

1. **Personalize.** Mention something specific about THEIR business
2. **Be brief.** 4-6 sentences max
3. **Lead with their pain.** Not your solution
4. **One CTA.** One ask. Not multiple ("call/email/visit website")
5. **Sign with you.** Your name, phone, email (not just "The Company")
6. **Test.** Try different angles, measure response rate

Follow these and your response rate will be 2-3x higher than generic outreach.
